Children's clothing is bought by adults for people who will have outgrown it by autumn. Sizes are counted in months. The category has organised itself around that fact — volume, replacement, the endless grid, the countdown banner. A boutique works the other way. Its value is in what it decided not to stock: a floor of Bonpoint, Petit Bateau, Bobo Choses, picked by someone with an eye. Most storefronts have no vocabulary for that.

Boy Meets Girl is a children's boutique in Coral Gables; bmgkids.com is its storefront, running on Shopify. The menu splits four ways — What's New, Brands, Shoes, then Baby and Kids. Brands is a directory, the house list alphabetised A–I, J–O, P–Z, every label with a collection of its own. Shoes are filed by stage rather than size: crib shoes, first walkers, toddler and kid. Sizing on a romper reads three month, six month, nine month.

The organising idea is that the label gets billing. Every card names the brand above the product; the product page opens with By Konges Sløjd before it says anything else. One typeface carries the whole site — Josefin Sans, tracked wide and set uppercase for headings, left plain everywhere else. Product rows alternate with three-up image blocks under centred titles. Sold-out pieces stay in the grid rather than vanishing, which is how a shop floor behaves.

“A boutique's value is in what it decided not to stock.”
