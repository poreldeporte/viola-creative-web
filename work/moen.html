A faucet is one of the few objects in a house touched every day and considered roughly twice: once when it is chosen, once when it drips. For most of the last century the choosing happened in a merchant's aisle, or was handed to a plumber, and the dripping was somebody else's problem entirely. Selling fixtures direct collapses that. One storefront now has to hold the person planning a bathroom and the person holding a worn cartridge.

Moen's store is the whole fixtures catalogue, sold direct. Kitchen and Bathroom divide into the shapes plumbing actually comes in — pulldown, pullout, bar and prep, pot fillers; centerset, widespread, wallmount — alongside showers, toilets, sinks, disposals and Smart Home. Named collections, Align through Weymouth, cut across rooms so a house can be furnished in one language. Faucet and shower finders stand in for the showroom question nobody can answer unprompted: what am I actually looking for?

The product page carries the idea. A model number sits beside the name, finish is a variant rather than a separate listing, and the tabs run Description, Documents, CAD, Details, Product Help, Reviews — so spec sheets, instruction sheets, exploded parts views and CAD files live inside the same object as the buy button. Service is indexed the same way, by component: valves, drains, cartridges, aerators, spouts, handles. A record, not a pitch.

“One storefront holds the person planning a bathroom and the person holding a worn cartridge.”
